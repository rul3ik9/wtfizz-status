Where the project is

All 35 parts of the game exist. A player can plant, water, pour, harvest, make cans, invent a flavour, name it, put it on the show board, build a monster, put it on display, and use the tower. Every one of the 15 player screens is built.

Playing it on 4 September found three faults that stopped a new player getting past the first few minutes, and all three are now fixed. Buttons in the world could be pressed from far too far away; the tutorial could switch off every button in the game at the moment you walked up to the thing it was pointing at; and the panel that opens each town building was drawing high above the roof, off the top of the screen, so the factory, lab, market, tower and gift stand all looked like they had nothing to interact with. The water tank also now shows how much water it holds and how much you are carrying, which was invisible before.

These were only found by playing. The fixes are confirmed in the build but have not yet been played through a second time, so this section will say “played end to end” only once that has happened.

The build is deliberately grey-box — real shapes and real rules, placeholder looks. Artwork is the next major stage, and it cannot start until the names and colours are approved. That is the single thing most worth your attention right now.

What we need from you

Three decisions, holding up four parts of the game. The first two block all artwork; the third affects how the game feels to play over time.

#What we needWhat it unblocks
1 Approval of the full name list — in one set, in writing. 45 foods, 46 monster pieces and 3 special recipes. Every one already has a finished Thai name in the game; they need your sign-off before anything is drawn. All artwork. Nothing can be modelled until this closes.
2 Approval of the 12 label colours. Each colour is tied to its own drink effect, so a drink always looks like its label. Can artwork and the drink effects.
3 One balance decision on how much a spare item is worth when converted back into cans. At the rates in the design document, players get slightly more back than they spend, which over time makes cans lose their value. The reward balance across the whole game.

A fourth, smaller one when convenient: two guidance lines say simply "ไร่" (farm) where every other zone is written out in full. They may well mean the player's own plot rather than the branded zone — we need your ruling on which, then we either rewrite them or leave them.

What happens next

  1. You approve the names and the colours. Everything visual waits on this — it is the critical path.
  2. Artwork begins — monster pieces, food items, decorations, can labels.
  3. You settle the one balance question on item values. We are adding measurements now so that decision can be made from real figures.
  4. The remaining in-progress items close. All are small and none need anything from you.
  5. Testing on real phones, then the launch checks.

The one thing worth doing today at no cost: the artist needs one instruction before modelling begins — monster pieces must share seven artwork sheets between them, with colour tinted onto plain grey shapes. Sending that now costs nothing. Sending it after the pieces are made means all 46 are redone.
